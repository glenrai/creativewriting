Step 2 · Criterion D: Using language

Dialogue punctuation

One line of speech can bring a character to life. Punctuate it correctly and examiners notice.

The six rules

  1. Spoken words, and the punctuation that belongs to them, go inside the speech marks.
    "The rain is here."
  2. When a speech tag follows, end the spoken words with a comma inside the marks.
    "The rain is here," Mum called.
  3. A question mark or exclamation mark stays, and the tag after it starts with a lowercase letter.
    "Look at me!" he shouted.
  4. When the tag comes first, put a comma before the opening mark and start the speech with a capital.
    Jay asked, "Can I keep them?"
  5. If one sentence is split by a tag, use commas on both sides and continue in lowercase. If a new sentence starts, use a full stop and a capital.
    "If you run," she warned, "you will slip."
  6. Each new speaker starts a new line (a new paragraph).
